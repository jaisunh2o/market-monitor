Climate and cost of living

Inflation and the physical climate, which move slowly but compound.

US inflation, year on year
CPI percent change on the same month a year earlier. Monthly, BLS data via a public dataset.
CO2 growth, year on year
Mauna Loa monthly mean minus the same month a year earlier, ppm. This removes the seasonal sawtooth, so the line shows how fast the atmosphere is accumulating CO2.
Global temperature anomaly
Degrees Celsius against the dataset baseline (NOAA series; baseline not stated in the file I read). Thin line: monthly. Thick line: trailing 12-month mean.

Where each number comes from
Which source feeds each view, how the views compare, and what I did about differences.
MeasureShown inSourceHow they compareStatus
  • Metals: monthly averages stop at April 2026 (World Bank), then daily readings run from late September; palladium has no history beyond a 2025 average.
  • Conflicts: every 2025 figure in the Security section can be traced to the 65 rows of the journal article's Appendix A. The non-state and one-sided figures come from the article text, and their lists are not loaded. There is no 2026 year-to-date conflict count; the preliminary table shows UCDP's trailing 12 months. Regional history is not available: the article's chart is an image.
  • The 26 Oct 2-year note's coupon is not published yet. The 30-year yield is only the 23 Oct reading.
  • The oil price chart uses news-reported December-contract futures settles from 20 Oct and grows with each refresh. The EIA spot series lags and feeds the spread chart, scorecard and correlations; gas and the VIX line also lag the news.
  • The website refreshes its EIA, FRED and public-dataset series by itself every day. News-site prices, the headline oil and S&P tiles, the conclusion and the guides are updated by hand by the author, so check the freshness table for their dates.
